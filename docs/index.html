How it works

  1. IOpen it fast. From the widget, the Quick Settings tile, an app shortcut, or the lock screen. Panic help never waits behind a PIN.
  2. IIAn urge, or panic. Ride out an urge with a timer and something to do, or breathe slowly with very large text and nothing to choose.
  3. IIITake your time. Breathe, ground yourself, read your own words, call someone. Afterwards, note how it went, only if you want to.

What it does

A few calm things, ready before you need them.

  • An urge timer Ten or twenty minutes to give an urge some time, a calm line to read, and a list of things to do while you wait. Five more minutes if you need them.
  • Breathing, never holding A slow circle to breathe with, 4 in and 6 out. Change the pace if you like: the out-breath is always longer, and there is never a hold.
  • 5-4-3-2-1 grounding Things you can see, touch, hear, smell and taste, one prompt at a time. Can't find one? Move on.
  • Your own words Cards written on a steadier day, shown during panic help. Kind built-in ones until you write your own.
  • Someone to call The people you trust as big buttons, a free support line for where you live, and your emergency number. Each opens your dialer.
  • Quicker than unlocking A Quick Settings tile that works on the lock screen, a two-button widget, and shortcuts for panic help or a 10 or 20 minute timer.
  • A quiet history A short note each time you use Kairos, just for you. The check-in afterwards is always optional, and you can skip it.
  • Gentle on the senses No sounds, no red, nothing that hurries you. Very large text, reduced motion, soft haptics if you want them, and TalkBack support.

Private by design

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, every feature, for everyone. There are no ads or in-app purchases, and the app doesn't contain ad or payment code. If you'd like to help, you can support it on Ko-fi.

Is Kairos medical care?

No. Kairos is a self-help tool. It can't diagnose or treat anything, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor or for emergency help. If you have chest pain, trouble breathing, or think you may be in danger, call your local emergency number.

Why doesn't the breathing ask me to hold my breath?

Holding can feel like one more thing to get right. Kairos only asks you to breathe in and breathe out, with the out-breath always longer than the in-breath. You can change the pace in settings, and go back to 4 in, 6 out at any time.

Will it open when my phone is locked?

Yes. Add the Kairos tile to Quick Settings, then swipe down and tap it, even from the lock screen. If you turn on Kairos's own app lock, it covers only your history and settings: panic help and urge mode always open straight away.

Which support lines does it know?

Kairos has a free support line built in for 21 countries and regions, picked from your phone's region, and you can add your own. Numbers can change, so please check the line works where you live. You choose the people you trust to call.

Does it work with Stagira?

If you use Stagira, the habit tracker, Kairos can offer to note an urge you rode out there as an urge resisted. Nothing is sent anywhere: Kairos opens Stagira on your phone, and Stagira asks you to confirm which goal it belongs to.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. Kairos is Android-only for now (Android 8.0 and later), and it's coming to Google Play soon.

Why “Kairos”?

Kairos (καιρός) is the Greek word for the right moment: the moment to pause, breathe, and let a wave pass.
